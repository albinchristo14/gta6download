## Designed after the consoles were frozen

PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X were specified years before launch, around eight cores of Zen 2 — 3.5 GHz in Sony's part, 3.8 GHz in Microsoft's — and that choice was made once and could not be revised afterwards. Intel's Core i5-12400F belongs to a different moment entirely: dated to 2022, placed in the mid tier, six cores and twelve threads, on a generation of desktop silicon that did not exist when the console hardware was locked.

This is the part of the argument that gets skipped most often. Console hardware is a snapshot; a desktop processor is not obliged to be from the same snapshot, and a mid-tier chip from two generations later can sit close to a flagship part from the design freeze on the strength of timing alone.

## What that makes it, in a hypothetical

A six-core Alder Lake chip is a plausible candidate in the thought experiment precisely because it is unremarkable: enough threads for the parallel work a streaming city generates, per-core behaviour from a much later design, and a motherboard generation newer than anything Sony or Microsoft could have specified for a 2020 launch. None of that becomes a claim about GTA 6, because there is no computer build for it to be a claim about. Strength without a specification is only potential.

## The socket question is the useful one

People rarely replace a processor alone. LGA 1700 machines can host a wide range of later Intel desktop parts, which means a 12400F bought in 2022 leaves a path open that a 2017 or 2018 board does not. For someone who would have upgraded anyway, the relevant comparison is not this chip against a console — it is whether the platform around the chip still has room in it.
