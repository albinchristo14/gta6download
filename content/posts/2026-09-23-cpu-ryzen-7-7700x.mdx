## A decision that should not start with this game

Nobody should buy this processor because a forum argument told them a game is coming, and nobody needs to. There is a respectable case for the Ryzen 7 7700X — AMD's high-tier 2023 part, eight cores and sixteen threads on Zen 4 — that has nothing to do with GTA 6, and it is worth stating before anything speculative, because the speculative part of the conversation is where money gets wasted.

## Two generations past the console silicon, with nothing to compare it to

Zen 4 sits behind neither PlayStation 5 nor Xbox Series X in the usual way those sentences work. Both consoles are Zen 2 designs with eight cores, hardware that was fixed years before launch and can never be revised; a 2023 desktop chip is two architecture generations later at the same core count, on parts that were never intended to be compared to a locked machine. The comparison is unavailable rather than unflattering, and that distinction matters here: there is no console version of a PC requirement to reverse-engineer, because there is no PC requirement.

## The platform is the part with a future

AM5 is where the argument lives. AMD's desktop sockets have historically stayed in use across several processor generations, which means a board bought today may host a chip that does not exist yet. That is the property a buyer is really purchasing: not performance in a specific unreleased title, but the option to change the processor later without changing the machine. It also carries a cost people underweight — memory, cooling and a motherboard all arrive in the same invoice, and none of them are cheaper for being sensible.

For someone replacing a PC that has reached the end of its platform anyway, this is a rational choice made for rational reasons. For someone whose current processor has eight cores and works, it is an upgrade bought against an imagined deadline.

## The deadline is not for your machine

The 19 November 2026 date belongs to PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. No PC edition has been announced, so no computer hardware of any vintage has a release date to be late for, and every page presenting a GTA 6 download as available now is a fiction with an installer attached. Where the money genuinely belongs first is a legitimate copy bought through a real store, which [the buying guide](/news/where-to-buy-gta-6-legitimately) works through edition by edition. If a computer version is ever confirmed, [the FPS estimator](/tools/fps-estimator) will weigh this processor against the rest of your build and show its working — that is the correct order for the purchase, and the reverse order is how people end up with new hardware and nothing to run on it.
