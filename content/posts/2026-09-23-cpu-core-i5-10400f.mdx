## The part that showed up in every build list

For a stretch of years the answer to a budget desktop question was the same handful of parts, and the Core i5-10400F was near the top of that list. Intel dated it to 2020 and filed it in the low tier with six cores and twelve threads — the same thread count as the console-class competition at the time, on a chip priced for people counting every line of a basket. It is in a lot of machines right now, which makes it one of the more useful processors to think about hypothetically.

## What the letter F removes

An F variant has no integrated graphics. That is the entire difference, and it is worth being precise about what it costs, because the loss is not performance. It is redundancy.

- No picture on the desktop if the discrete card dies, is removed, or has a driver that will not load.
- No quick route to a working display while a new GPU is being fitted.
- None of the encoder or assist features that ride along with processor graphics.
- A marginally lower price, which is the reason any of that is accepted.

For a machine whose graphics card is the component doing the work, none of this is a handicap in normal use. It becomes one at the exact moment something stops working.

## The part that decides is the one you cannot see here

A CPU with twelve threads is not the interesting constraint in a budget build. The graphics card is, and so is whatever the card is plugged into. Rockstar has announced GTA 6 for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S on 19 November 2026 and has announced nothing for computers, so no specification exists for a 10400F to satisfy or to fail; the first half of the question is still open. [The PC download status page](/gta-6-download-pc) tracks what has actually been said about a computer release, which is nothing to date.
