Thirteen years separate the two games, and the differences that matter on launch day are mostly not in the trailer. This comparison sticks to what is verifiable: what Rockstar has confirmed about GTA 6, what its own record with GTA 5 establishes, and where the two clearly diverge. Anything that has not been confirmed is marked as reported or expected rather than stated as fact.

## The comparison that affects your wallet

| | GTA 5 | GTA 6 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Console launch | September 2013 (PS3 / Xbox 360) | 19 November 2026 (PS5 / Xbox Series X\|S) |
| PC launch | April 2015 — roughly 18 months later | Not announced at all |
| Launch price (US) | $59.99 | $79.99 Standard, $99.99 Ultimate |
| Physical copy | Disc | Download code in a box, no disc |
| Multiplayer at launch | GTA Online, free with the game and later bundled standalone | No GTA 6 Online announced as a product |
| Platforms skipped | Wii U rejected | PS3 / Xbox 360 / PS4 / Xbox One all unsupported |

Three of those rows are decisions people are still getting wrong in September 2026, so they are worth unpacking.

## The PC gap is the biggest difference

GTA 5 came to PC in April 2015, about eighteen months after consoles, with requirements that assumed a CPU with AVX support and 4 GB minimum RAM — hardware that was already ageing on arrival, which is exactly why the port worked so widely and still runs today.

GTA 6 has no equivalent date. Rockstar has confirmed PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S and nothing else, and has published no PC requirements, price or store page. The common assumption that "it always comes to PC eventually" is a pattern, not a promise: Red Dead Redemption 2 arrived on PC in 2019 after an eleven-month console gap, and Max Payne 3 in 2012 was also delayed but not skipped. What the precedent supports is a wait of roughly a year to two. What it does not support is a download link, a Steam page, or a spec table — and any site offering one of those is filling the silence with invented content. See the honest version on our [system requirements page](/gta-6-system-requirements).

The consequence for buyers is real: if you are a PC player deciding whether to buy a console, you are being asked to pay for a platform on the assumption of a release date nobody has announced.

## The price rose, and the disc disappeared

GTA 5's $59.99 was the standard of 2013. GTA 6's $79.99 Standard is the current generation's premium tier for a game of this scale, with the $99.99 Ultimate adding in-game content such as clothing, tattoos, vehicles and weapons. Notably, neither tier buys earlier access: the official materials present them as content tiers, not access tiers.

The bigger change is physical. GTA 5's boxed copy was the game. GTA 6's boxed copy is packaging for a download code, a decision Rockstar confirmed for launch that drew enough backlash that it publicly responded, and enough retailer hesitation that some chose not to stock it. For a buyer, that means:

- The resale, lending and "keep a copy forever" properties of GTA 5's box are gone.
- A data-capped or slow connection is now a reason to buy differently, not just to pay more.
- The disc-drive version of your console no longer guarantees anything.

## A different kind of open world, with a smaller claim of certainty

GTA 5 shipped three playable protagonists switchable mid-mission, one contiguous map of Los Santos and Blaine County, and no single-player online requirement. GTA 6's confirmed material so far shows two protagonists, Jason and Lucia, in and around Leonida with Vice City at the centre, and a map that reporting — not Rockstar — describes as roughly three times GTA 5's with 30-plus named areas. Its creator was described by a former Rockstar developer as "like GTA but on acid", which is a comment from someone who worked with him, not a feature list.

One structural fact does come from primary sources and should shape expectations: Rockstar's Rockstar Integrated Development Environment (RIDE) has been in rollout for years, which is the same infrastructure argument that explains a two-delay schedule rather than a rushed one. The game has slipped from 2025 to May 2026 to November 2026 — and history says Rockstar does not move a date without believing it needs to.

## What carries over, and what does not

- **Preserved:** Rockstar's structure of single-player plus a long-lived online service, its habit of patching and supporting a release for a decade, and the fact that the online component is where the money is made after launch.
- **Gone:** cheap physical ownership, backward-compatible console purchases, and any assumption that a game you pre-ordered on one platform family will ever appear on another.
- **Still unknown, honestly:** PC timing, install size, frame-rate targets, whether an online component ships with the game or later, and whether the map's reported scale survives into the release build.

If you only take one buying decision from this: pre-ordering GTA 6 is a normal purchase for the two platforms it is confirmed on, and an act of faith on any other.
